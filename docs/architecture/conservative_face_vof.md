# Conservative Face-Stream Transport Contract

This document defines the architectural contract for face-stream VoF transport. Unlike swept-volume methods, face-stream transport advances phase inventories directly on fixed native mesh faces, decoupling donor-cell admission from complex swept-solid intersection geometry.

## Architectural Approach

- **Face-Stream Mapping:** The transport mechanism maps donor-to-recipient fluxes as integrated volume streams on canonical faces. Each canonical face is handled once, regardless of mesh refinement levels.
- **Backend Selection:** Two primary backends are supported: `DonorCell` (algebraic inventory transport) and `GeometricFace` (enhanced geometric reconstruction). Both share the same canonical face-ledger interface, enabling modular backend selection.
- **Algebraic Consistency:** The framework enforces conservation algebraically by ensuring incident cells share a consistent face flux, eliminating internal transfer errors across coarse/fine refinement boundaries.

## Physical and Numerical Constraints

- **Source-Free Advection:** Initial implementation focuses on source-free, incompressible flows with impermeable boundaries.
- **State Preservation:** The transport mechanism respects independent phase inventories. It does not perform inventory clipping or fraction renormalization to fix balance errors; instead, it uses synchronized balance checks to detect and repair roundoff-induced defects.
- **Physical Gates:** Transport is governed by strict physical occupancy gates and extensive conservation requirements. Inaccurate transport endpoints reject the candidate solver trial rather than polluting the accepted state.

## Integration with Multiphysics

- **Momentum Coupling:** Momentum transport is coupled to the mass fluxes generated by the face-stream mechanism. This ensures velocity-field consistency with the phase interface.
- **Surface Geometry:** `GeometricFace` backends provide canonical interface surface descriptors (area, normal, centroid) to capillary and surface-tension consumers, maintaining geometric consistency with the transport planes.

## Scope and Verification
The face-stream backends are designed as capability extensions to the standard swept-volume approach. Verification focuses on:
- Convergence of phase-interface transport in complex topologies.
- Preservation of inventory conservation under extreme density ratios.
- Correctness of mass and momentum fluxes across adaptive mesh seams.
