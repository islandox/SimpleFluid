# Spatial adaptive closure

Closure grading uses complete closed-contact neighborhoods: shared faces, edges,
vertices, nonconforming edge endpoints and supported periodic images. A spatial
broad phase only identifies candidates; it does not establish contact or authorize
refinement by itself. Connected-domain provenance keeps coincident independent
meshes separate.

The discovery foundation lives below native publication. Hierarchy construction
requires closure before a native halo exists, and the published face-neighbor halo
alone does not establish edge/vertex completeness. The provider contract declares
contact normalization and conservative represented-cell image bounds. Unsupported
capabilities are explicit. Cartesian logical intervals are used for additional
culling only when their complete frame matches and their physical embedding is
proved injective. Periodic images retain their physical-to-contact displacement.
Mixed periodic routes retain their existing rejection boundary.

The provider-bounds foundation `ClosedContactIndex` owns compact
key/domain/revision/owner/image metadata and a local height-balanced tree. It does not retain expanded geometry or a global leaf
catalog. Startup performs one provider bounds evaluation per cell and O(N log N)
metadata insertion; queries and mutations operate on the tree. Closed overlap keeps
face/edge/vertex equality. Results are deduplicated in canonical key order and
borrow entries until erasure/replacement. Returned vectors belong to the caller,
which must account for overlapping outputs in its enclosing budget. An optional
scratch cap bounds simultaneous result construction. Index live capacity and
construction overlap are capped independently of monotonically accumulated
admission observations. The directory cover uses a bounded frontier of actual
tree subtrees: it splits every subtree containing more than one owner or domain,
then refines homogeneous subtrees toward a target of eight fragments. Mandatory
owner/domain splits can produce more than eight. Covers are conservative; they
may identify extra candidates, while their owner and domain remain exact. A
homogeneous tree does not require a leaf scan to construct this frontier.
Operations preserve caller rounding and exception flags.

Ordinary coordinate bounds use the immutable canonical world-coordinate view,
including the original pre-rebase source and its captured arithmetic environment.
They enclose the represented convex hull without display-coordinate conversion or
point-export publication. They are not, by themselves, a proof of every contact
accepted by the legacy rounded edge-index arithmetic. Contact discovery must retain
complete canonical incidence or establish the corresponding conservative acceptance
bound before using this broad phase to exclude candidates. A false completeness
claim must never replace a missing remote response or unresolved geometric predicate.

The shared closure-record provider preserves the existing certificate validation,
lossless axis spans, trace provenance, physical-point membership and grading rules.
It supports both the legacy wire codec and direct owning record preparation. The
codec retains field order and scalar bytes. Local preparation admits owned fields
before allocation and shares the existing nested geometry-admission boundary;
outer live-byte observations are not substitutes for those construction caps.
Point-independent trace inventories share immutable owners only when every
ordered stem, depth, split and terminal field agrees. Point-restricted face traces
keep their existing ownership. Records still expose complete trace evidence, and
encoding selects that evidence without changing packet contents. Mutable record
metadata uses stable pages; key lookup borrows each page's complete cell key,
which avoids a second retained ancestry copy.

A new distributed hierarchy bootstraps complete physical contact incidence through
the canonical support/edge machinery and retains its accepted contact session.
The index used for exclusion is built from the actual normalized incidence points,
not merely the provider hull. This retains legacy edge-index admissions while the
provider hull interface remains a broad-phase building block. Production halo
ownership discovery uses `ContactCoverIndex`: it borrows the distinct normalized
point coordinates and interned domains owned by point groups and stores separate
flat leaf and branch metadata. Its bounded frontier consists of actual subtrees;
mixed-domain subtrees split regardless of the soft fragment target. Candidate
copies rebind their borrowed handles to their own point and domain owners before
use. The provider-bounds index remains a separate foundation, not this halo index.

An adaptation plan forks candidate-private editable indexes from the accepted
session. Immutable identity, geometry-record and incidence bodies may be shared;
the candidate is independently admitted and cannot mutate the accepted session.
The hierarchy stages sparse leaf changes over its accepted immutable page root.
Initial user changes and closure splits are routed as retired key/revision and
inserted-key deltas. A sparse owner and revision overlay sits over the accepted
ancestry directory. Its tombstones never fall through to an obsolete base entry;
addresses are not used as ancestry. Providers with the explicit subset-repack
capability normalize only dirty records. The final state is registered after
convergence rather than once per refinement pass.

Ancestry directories retain exact keys, owner and revision in immutable ordered
fact pages. Key buffers are admitted before they are copied, and clean page
subtrees are shared across generations. Observed cell GIDs are a separate dense
numeric projection in shard-key order, so a prefix shift rewrites addresses
without rewriting ancestry facts. Generic revisions remain literal full-width
values; Product-family rows may project the current topology epoch only through
a private token validated against the exact family layout. A candidate directory
reconciles only changed facts, holds inherited fact credit in its source overlap
until publication, and transfers only surviving shared facts.

Authority membership is established by `kernel.surfaces(source_leaf)` in a
transient copy. A retained polyhedral leaf can already have physical support
records whose keys and boundary aliases differ from the mapped authority view;
the cache must preserve those source records and must not bypass the mapping
because the retained surface list is nonempty.

Actual Cartesian providers may certify immutable source ranges through an
explicit provider capability. A public provider label, descriptor, hash or
recipe-relative slot cannot authorize that representation. Sources retain their
complete domain, ancestry ranges, revisions, evaluated axes and arithmetic
environment. Support lookup filters original full canonical keys; logical
adjacency or hull overlap never establishes complete membership. An uncertified
inverse lookup regenerates its authenticated contributing ranges and filters
their full generated keys.

The original complete support overlay and complete line query run before a
recipe is certified. Its complete ordered output identities must agree exactly
with the proposed representation. Translated images, inverse periodic
translation, projection ties, winding, support order and the existing layer
limit remain part of that proof. A mismatch keeps explicit evidence; a
Cartesian label alone never permits an implicit answer.

Owner manifests bind the complete contributing inventory to the source versions
and membership generation. Every owner supplies a completion, including an empty
owner. The manifest contains authenticated source facts, rather than a complete
cell-neighbor or cell-pair table. Existing v2 line-data packets retain their
canonical bytes. Separate typed control messages carry source dependencies and
their context, layer, wave, page and ordered full-value bindings. Missing,
duplicate, stale, reordered or truncated controls cannot become empty results.

Regular edits enter certification after the existing support/line fixed point
and before their changed rows publish. New immutable source inventories retain
the exact captured evaluation facts of unchanged sources; authenticated retired
and inserted ancestry ranges determine the new active membership. Changed
sources retain the caller environment under which their original kernels ran.
Advancing the inventory generation does not retag old numerical facts with new
controls. Old/new supports, then old/new lines and every affected tile, retain
the established invalidation order. Dirty line recipes are checked against the
completed real-query results, and unchanged certificates require the same full
dependency proof. Numerically exceptional neighborhoods keep explicit results.

Complete-support coverage stores handles only for actual intersections. Each
row grows under admission that includes simultaneous old and new allocations;
exterior supports allocate no coverage rows. The unchanged pair enumeration
retains its conservative cardinality/work ceiling independently of this sparse
storage. Constructor replay leases one live pair operation at a time, then
retains each actual output owner before releasing its construction envelope.
Partition validation leases its conservative envelope for the actual covering
pieces. Callback failure unwinds the same lease; it cannot publish a partial
support. These bounds avoid reserving all potential pair constructions at once;
a conservative represented-rectangle index supplies candidate search where its
arithmetic certificate applies. It uses the actual immutable four-corner faces,
a common physical plane, a bounded dyadic coordinate lattice and captured
round-to-nearest controls. Closed bounds retain edge/corner candidates, and
geometry predicates retain their original occurrence order. The initial
certificate uses binary 64-bit long-double precision, quantum exponents from
-128 through 128, and scaled coordinates of magnitude at most 2^14; unsupported
shapes, scales or controls use exhaustive enumeration. A control change during
emission resumes exhaustive ordering before any subsequent pair can be skipped.
The existing cardinality/work ceiling remains independent of this optimization.
A changed support is reconstructed from its complete, authenticated owner
membership. Dirty supports are fetched in byte-bounded pages and preserve member
arrival order. During replay, each affected tile first retains compact line slots, physical
endpoint slots and receipt bits. A full working layer is materialized when a
reply differs from the ordered endpoint baseline. Endpoint-only replies still
undergo the original polygon, support and error checks, including translated-image
validation and the original inverse-translation arithmetic. Cached point
candidates are assembled in the legacy authority, line-key and image/edge order.
Completed staging owners retire before new incidence rows are allocated.

Changed tiles and their line subscriptions drive layered line replay;
the resulting rows identify all affected owned cells, including an unchanged
coarse leaf whose newly incident point changes its row without changing its leaf
revision. Leaf revision and incidence generation are separate observations.
Unchanged records can therefore remain shared while changed point neighborhoods
and the associated pair requirements are recomputed. Empty owners participate in
the same collective phases. A failed plan discards the candidate and its overlay,
leaving the accepted hierarchy, cache and source observations intact.

The persistent support directory can encode one Cartesian domain and region prefix.
It packs the three coordinate words, axis and level into a private selector when
each coordinate fits eight bits, the level fits five, and the original domain
fits the frame. A sparse, persistent presence mask distinguishes an interned
key from a representable key that was never inserted. Other keys retain literal
storage. Comparisons and transport use the complete original bytes. A detached
provenance page retains its own frame for packed supports and captures physical
pool pages for literal supports.

Explicit incidence identity pools store full values in pages of sixteen records. Byte,
cell and point keys use templated hashing policies and flat hash-slot lookup with
full-value collision checks; plane, normal and image keys retain tree lookup. Point hashing isolates and
restores the caller's floating-point environment while emitting canonical words. Hash buckets and stable pool slots are private
references, not numerical or canonical ordering. Forks share existing page owners
and copy lookup metadata, then append to a private page instead of changing a
shared page. A flat fork admits that shared body in its own arena. When the
source uses a borrowed budget, destroying the source pool retires its control
credit even if the fork still reads the page at the same address.
Hashing and dynamic-payload accounting are template policies; forks preserve
their configuration. Shared plane payload remains in the separate identity ledger.
Fork overlap includes every current-source pool credit object. A credit required
by an inherited row is transferred from source-only overlap to persistent
borrowed admission, rather than charged twice. Credits originating in an earlier
ancestor remain distinct allocations even when they pin the same immutable page.
These pool pages are independent of PLIC point-snapshot paging. Per-cell point
membership uses sorted pool-slot reference counts, so removal of one contributing
incidence does not remove a point still supplied by another. Canonical contact
rows retain checked 32-bit references, or a 64-bit alternative, into a frozen
directory shared by each changed-row batch. The directory pins only referenced
point pages and their arena credits, independently of the mutable pool lookup.
Logical insertion slots and captured references are separate: a fork may seal a
partially filled page, so insertion slots cannot be decoded by division by the
page size. Rows preserve full physical-identity ordering and the existing
normalization and duplicate checks. Normalized references share the physical
array while every pair is equal; a separate reference array is retained only
when normalization differs. Standalone/manual rows retain their owning adapter.

Certified Cartesian point storage uses immutable canonical component tables and
checked compact selectors, with complete explicit identities for exceptions.
Components come from actual validated point identities; reading them performs no
normalization or geometry evaluation. A source-backed directory preserves logical
pool slots while using a distinct captured-reference namespace from physical
sixteen-record pages. A new source snapshot must reproduce every referenced full
identity before publication. Previously published rows and captured physical
references keep their original snapshots and pages after edits or source
destruction; they never redirect to a newer generation.

Repeated captures share a flat directory of immutable value blocks. Unchanged
component and point indices retain their meanings and widths; new blocks append
only new components, selectors and lookup entries. Before reuse, the new capture
checks every prior full identity against the actual current values. Value blocks
contain no older snapshot or owner-manifest binding. Current certificates retain
their own proof, while surviving older rows and recipes keep the exact historical
proof they require. Indexed reads select blocks without walking a recursive
snapshot ancestry chain.

Point-row iteration and indexed access return immutable identity values. Consumers
that need a stable borrow retain an exact row/page/generation locator and resolve
it synchronously. There is no hidden expanded point array or proxy conversion to
a reference whose lifetime ends with the accessor call. The cover index and
physical pair evaluation follow those locators without copying resident full-key
arrays.

Each arena independently admits shared point pages once. Published directories
retain the originating arena until their last row/view is destroyed. Forked
sessions separately admit borrowed directory and originating credit-object
metadata, without charging their page payload twice. Failed row publication
destroys partial buffers before returning live admission; cumulative spending
remains unchanged. An unchanged delta constructs no frozen directory.

Resident `PooledTile` records keep only the cell roles local to that resident,
while complete authenticated membership remains in the authority registries.
Normals and image vectors refer to pooled full-precision values that preserve
signed zero; they do not establish authority. Support groups retain their
generation and zero, one or two members inline, with an immutable shared spill for
larger groups. Empty groups retain generation tombstones. The owner-local reverse
support index likewise stores up to two cell slots inline and shares immutable
larger spills. Full member fields and canonical member ordering are preserved.

Persistent tile bodies occupy immutable pages of sixteen records with ordered
flat rings. Actual slots and terminal offsets select checked 32-bit storage or
the 64-bit alternative; cell presence is separate from its index, and emission
ordinals retain their full width. The construction draft is temporary. A complete
pending page batch is sealed before replay can read its bodies.
Candidate-private indexed entries hold a page reference, initialization state
and optional layer history. Layerless tiles allocate no history owner. Forks
share immutable body pages, independently admit their arena credits, and rebind
entry references. Histories detach only on mutation; changed-entry snapshots
retain the old bodies and histories until contribution removal completes.
Vector-backed replay inputs explicitly pin their layer owner through replacement
or pruning; page-backed inputs retain their page reference.

After exact source-point certification, a derivable Cartesian tile ring retains
its immutable source recipe instead of an individual ring in the page's flat
array. Other polygon degrees and nonmatching outputs retain their explicit rings.
Scalars, source orientation and emission identity remain unchanged. Replacing a
private body does not rewrite an older page still borrowed by a contribution,
accepted state or escaped view.

Retirement releases a private page when no tile/view needs it. If an accepted
source still retains the immutable body, its candidate body credit moves to a
weak-owner table until physical expiry. Re-adoption transfers that existing
credit back without charging the body twice. Failed metadata admission leaves
the original page owner intact. Fork overlap counts distinct source credit
objects and directory metadata; shared history bodies are admitted once by the
candidate rather than added again as source-only metadata. Diagnostics separate
occupied/live records, page/ring capacities, history storage, credit objects and
deferred body storage.

The persistent tile index stores each full support/emission key with its entry
in immutable ordered pages. A batch sorts its insertions and retired supports,
builds the initial root or edits the affected paths of an existing root, then
publishes the result. Successful publication destroys both staging buffers
before releasing their live admission. A rejected batch preserves the old root.

When bootstrap starts with an empty tile index, every published tile is new.
The first replay layer reads its keys by ordinal in support/emission order, taking
each key by value so that replacing an entry cannot invalidate a borrowed key.
This avoids building a journal of empty predecessors and a duplicate pending
set. Updates to populated indexes retain the predecessor evidence for removing
old contributions. Later periodic rounds retain their ordered pending sets.

Line replay streams edge edits and batches tile delivery in bounded pages.
Stored subscribers use checked 32-bit fields for support and endpoint slots,
emission and edge when representable, with a full-width spill otherwise; the
wire retains its 64-bit fields. A query materializes the subscribers of one
complete line transiently and preserves the existing arithmetic and ordering. A
certified layer containing only its intrinsic endpoints can be represented
implicitly; its subscriptions and output are reconstructed from the intrinsic
loop when needed. A private v2 line wave checks the complete subscriber identity,
rejects duplicate completions and verifies the completion count for each source.
A completed page alone is not proof that the full wave arrived. Certified
Cartesian subscribers instead use shared actual-axis maps and ordered
source-coordinate families with range/mask membership. Nonforward complete
results, including coarse/fine intermediate points, use certified result
selectors. Neither representation retains an expanded subscriber or output-point
mirror. A synchronous read reconstructs one record in the original order from
its exact source dependencies. Certification and storage observations include
every stored completed layer, not only the currently effective final layer.
Each recipe retains the exact layer and full completed-output witness of its
bucket. Genuine exceptions retain the explicit form.

Published family descriptors use one checked 32-bit or 64-bit private-index
alternative per recipe. Fixed and mapped components share one selector array
with explicit tags; numerical components and wire/generation words retain their
precision and limits. Small memberships live inline, while general masks,
ranges and result selectors use exact-capacity flat buffers. Complete prototype
bytes share a catalog table by full equality. Construction drafts and reusable
per-line metadata scratch remain invocation-local and fully admitted alongside
old and new owners until whole-pass validation succeeds.

Tile residents own compact result receipts for remote authority answers. A
receipt is issued only after the original reply, control, member, chunk and
completion checks succeed. It binds the original request endpoints; v2 reply
data does not contain those endpoint fields. Current read context and effective
layer are distinct from the actual stored kernel-witness layer. If an intrinsic
endpoint-only answer was previously discarded, an on-demand bounded control
phase reads that already completed authority result before the original layer
rebuild. It does not rerun a line query, normalization or translation. Empty
participants complete the same control phases; zero logical requests do not
imply zero envelope traffic when the phase is invoked.

Saved loops share exact ordered edge-result parts and select the physical
contour slice for periodic images. Full loop replay precedes release of old
input, output, endpoint and result arrays. Shared layer bodies detach before
sealing even when their allocation budget matches the editing branch. Compact
views retain exact proof dependencies; temporary expanded receipt results
retire after successful sealing. Uniform endpoint-only tiles retain no
permanent receipt inventory.

Before sealing an owning receipt, the exchange may lend its authenticated reply
to a synchronous consumer after all terminal, peer and global completion-count
checks succeed. The borrow and its spans last only for that callback. A tile
without a materialized body consumes a reply only when it matches the exact
ordered original endpoints and their context, layer, membership and point-pool
binding. Consumption records the receive and dirty-subscription counts once,
then destroys the pending result before releasing its live allocation credit.
Declined replies follow the owning-receipt path. If a later edge requires a
saved layer, authenticated cached-proof recovery can add control waves; it does
not replay the earlier receive or dirty-subscription counts.

Pooled-line point packets append their canonical body and the common geometry
envelope directly to admitted output capacity. The append checks the complete
frame before modifying the destination and creates no temporary key or packet
owner. Packet bytes and the caller's canonical scalar evaluation environment
remain those of the owning encoder; only the eliminated heap scratch loses its
prospective admission charge.

Canonical subface queries share the final physical loop with incidence replay.
Delivery captures both incident keys, revisions, owners and original source
surface ordinals after the complete support receipt has been authenticated.
The local surface supplies its native face locator and boundary name. A foreign
incident keeps its source surface locator until native publication fetches that
source. The piece keeps the authority's support, domain, generation, emission
order, and periodic displacement. Provenance retains the original orientation-independent
physical face key. It stores groups of eight bytes with a nonzero-byte mask when
that representation is smaller, and keeps dense keys in their original encoding.
Publication writes the authenticated support and physical key, with their original
length words, directly into admitted destination capacity. An escaped piece can
reproduce these bytes after its session is released.

After line completion, a piece references its existing intrinsic tile page, an
exact certified source loop, or the immutable buffer transferred from the final
explicit replay output. A detached point namespace uses the certified source
prefix and captured physical page ranges. This keeps the exact original point
identities available after the incidence session or hierarchy is released.
Candidates share unchanged piece and provenance pages. The face index selects
pieces by owned persistent key and source surface ordinal and reports the sign
of the requested incidence.

Native point recapture authenticates the directory's accepted snapshot by its
exact shared owner and immutable slot namespace. The new snapshot shares
independent value blocks through an immutable block directory and indexes only
the appended axis values. Existing point and component slots retain their
original identities. External point readers still verify their complete
prefix and routing before publication. Escaped references to the physical
suffix keep their original pages and admission credits across replacement.

Scalar and complete-point lookup use persistent ordinal indexes over the same
immutable value blocks. Appends copy only the affected index paths. Native
point indexes store compact ordinals and read the original complete identities
for ordering and equality. The configured deterministic hash validates routing;
a collision never replaces identity comparison. Generic hashless and custom-hash
policies retain their complete-value lookup and exact supplied routing checks.
Index pages and their old/new overlap participate in admission and physical census.
A complete identity query performs O(log P * log B) work for P points in B value
blocks, including selector reconstruction and component-directory descent.

Geometry materialization admits its work and output separately. The canonical
identity uses the support-normalized intrinsic loop before edge enrichment.
The returned face keeps the final first-cell physical loop. Node and face GIDs
belong to native publication and are assigned through its existing authority
rules.

Migration planning validates a destination for every owned leaf and scans those
leaves for outgoing records. The candidate session retires moved memberships at
old owners and inserts them at new owners, including when key and revision stay
the same. Owner routing and dirty support and line state follow the new partition.
Partition binding checks the communicator, map and cell counts, then validates
GID order, owner and applicable family for each owned leaf. The exact native
path authenticates the publication receipt and rechecks the current partition
control under the geometry-cache lock. Compatibility binding requests each owned
cell's geometry and checks equivalence before reusing the cache.

One stable typed point-group catalog owns local and remote fan slices. Domains
and complete normalized identities refer to immutable pooled facts; periodic
remote physical identities remain separate where required. Canonical rows import
only their referenced point pages, while owning inputs use packed fallback pages.
The cover index resolves coordinates from those stable identity owners rather
than retaining another coordinate array. No resident serialized point-key mirror
is retained.

Lookup hashes never decide identity. Communication and evaluation scheduling
retain the complete legacy framed-key order: encoded domain length and bytes,
then encoded point-key length and bytes, including the platform's existing
`char` comparison. Inline canonical emission uses bounded stack storage and
restores the caller's floating-point environment. Arbitrary bounded incoming
key bytes remain unmatched probes rather than acquiring a new decoding failure.

Immutable fan blocks contain compact local binding/point slots and remote
record/physical-point slots, with a checked wide alternative. Bindings pin the
exact row and incidence generation, so reusing a row slot cannot redirect an
old occurrence. A candidate stages only touched fan edits, preserving survivors
then append order and remote response order. Slice ownership releases obsolete
bindings independently while other slices retain their shared block. Old pair
obligations are collected before edits. Dirty/query leases and owning fan views
delay group, binding and point reuse until their readers have retired.

Forks establish the candidate incidence pool before importing group facts, then
rebind every cover to candidate-owned facts. Body and ledger identities govern
shared admission; containing objects and embedded ledgers do not share a dedupe
identity merely because their addresses coincide. The borrowed group/fact/fan
payload inventory reports actual object and buffer extents independently from
admission allowances and explicitly excludes unmeasured allocator, control-block
and tree-node overhead. It does not by itself inventory every native or legacy
Halo owner. Allocating ordered-query leases and empty group-constructor owners
participate in collective admission before communication begins.

A distributed binary rank-cover tree stores bounds only. It returns overlapping
owner fragments; complete normalized point keys then go directly to those owners.
Exact matches return all associated cell/revision/physical-point incidences, or an
explicit empty completion. Cell records are fetched by matched owner, key and
revision and reused while valid in the candidate session. Private protocol v2
binds messages to geometry epoch, topology, partition, transaction, closure round,
query and page; source identity and the complete geometry observation are also
pinned and checked by the hierarchy plan. Sender, member, completion and terminal
counts are authenticated. Missing messages cannot become empty neighborhoods.
Pages and decoded records share a live admission budget with the retained index,
fans, pair table, overlay and cached records.

The evaluator builds unique cell pairs before numerical comparison. Its candidate
table stores exact paired local record IDs compactly when both fit 32 bits and
uses full-width storage otherwise; hashes never establish pair identity. A fully
admitted sorted buffer restores the original ascending pair order before any
numerical comparison. Dirty-point receipts borrow stable point-map keys and are
retired as their pair IDs are gathered. Empty-local point groups can then retire,
while their old record IDs remain available for requirement cleanup. Owned/owned
pairs contribute both local requirements; owned/remote pairs contribute only the
owned requirement. Point-independent span/trace work is reused across shared
contacts; physical-point-dependent trace membership is still checked at each
required incidence. Actual performed comparisons consume the contact work cap for
that closure round, while logical point-incidence pairs are reported separately. A new geometry
refinement pass begins a new round; paging within a round never resets its work
cap or cumulative admission counters.

These closure representation changes preserve numerical exports and reconstruction
behavior. The public cell-view API and explicit materialization boundary are
described in [the general VoF ownership contract](general_vof_implementation.md).
The candidate arena admits actual capacities and the
one-time source/candidate metadata overlap and
retains charges for shared bodies once in that candidate. The source-overlap
reservation is released at publication, while the accepted candidate retains its
own live charges. Packet, decoded-record and returned-result lifetimes are charged
while they overlap. Temporary decoded planes use a scoped ledger; canonical
planes adopted by the incidence pools use the session ledger. Retired immutable
bodies that remain pinned by an accepted state keep their admitted credit through
weak-owner tracking. Expired owners are reaped at operation boundaries, so live
statistics and storage can remain conservative until the next reap; cumulative
admissions are never refunded. Typed-container node charges use the actual stored
types; correcting those charges is an accounting change, not evidence of fewer
allocations. Existing opaque provider and PLIC geometry construction and codec
operations retain their own nested admission policies; the outer accounting is
not a bound on all allocator activity or process RSS. `storage()` includes the
retained contact cache in its owned-byte estimate without adding the bound Data
admission a second time.

Mesh geometry snapshots are immutable roots shared with FVM cell views and PLIC
source references. Snapshot acquisition authenticates the actual source binding
and arithmetic controls, and admits construction, cache metadata and replacement
overlap before publishing a new root. Hierarchy residency leases pin only the
allocation arena, never the halo or hierarchy. Rebinding copies the mutable cache
shell while sharing immutable roots and transferring already admitted residency;
adaptation starts a new cache for the changed geometry version. Snapshot and
source views can outlive the hierarchy without retaining mutable transaction
state. The borrowed census follows snapshot catalogs and their exact external
recipe owners without locking weak caches or preparing numerical data.

Source inventories, evaluated-axis maps, point directories, certificates and
exceptions participate in the borrowed retention census. Shared immutable payload
and referencing metadata are separate. Each candidate independently admits shared
source bodies once in its own arena; the accepted arena's live header, lookup
nodes, credit tokens and tile-source metadata remain part of simultaneous
overlap. Exact owner identities govern transfers from overlap to an escaping
lease. Ancestor credit metadata remains distinct even when it retains the same
body. Buffer capacity, old/new replacements and complete-group replay scratch
are admitted before allocation; successful retirement never refunds cumulative
spending. Live allocation traffic, census extents, admission and RSS are separate
measurements.

Canonical pooled plane identities and the existing PLIC ledger have separate
borrowed root access. Their required plane owners and ledger pins are reported
independently from contact-container payload. Temporary support-fetch and decode
ledgers retain their operation-local lifetimes; certification does not reset a
production ledger or refund cumulative admission.

Returned row vectors retain their caller-owned buffer credit only after a
successful return. A later certification or collective failure destroys their
current buffers before releasing live admission, including when compaction
replaces a vector. Temporary patches and retired-key precharges use the same
destruction-before-release discipline. Cumulative construction charges remain
spent on every failed path.

The old closure implementation remains an explicit private test oracle, never a
production fallback. The standalone closure call still creates a complete
bootstrap for that invocation. The hierarchy session and its incremental replay
are separate from that standalone invocation; neither is evidence of broader
numerical or physical qualification.

## Cubic halo storage

The cubic Cartesian fixtures divide a uniform grid into four equal slabs. The
explicit-row fixture supplies integer corner incidences. The canonical fixtures
also construct and retain the incidence cache from native Cartesian recipes.
The cap applies per rank to live arena admission. Peak includes temporary
reservations for encoding and decoding, which can use all remaining headroom.
Retained bytes measure the live charge after the first refinement query.
Cumulative admission includes retired reservations and can exceed the cap.

All three probes below use a 64 MiB per-rank cap. The N16 canonical and N32
explicit-row measurements use GCC Debug. Full canonical N32 uses GCC RelWithDebInfo.
A separate four-rank registration exercises the same full canonical N32 oracle at 96 MiB.
The table gives **peak / retained bytes**.
